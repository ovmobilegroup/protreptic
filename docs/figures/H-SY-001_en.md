### Mode 1: Wood Trust Establishment Method (M381)

**Instantiation**
> "Removing a tree is equivalent to giving a reward." — Lord Shang ordered wood placed at southern gate, offering fifty gold to move it. When someone did and received the reward, laws were promulgated with authority established through trust.

**Unique Perspective**
- **Government Credibility Cornerstone**: Wood-moving reward experiment verifies government credit, establishing consistent order enforcement
- **Promise Fulfillment Mechanism**: Once promised, must be fulfilled; otherwise generous rewards are given, embodying the central role of 'trust' in governance
- **Order Enforcement**: Government promises and laws must be executed consistently

**Modern Applications**

## Historical Significance

Lord Shang (c. 370-338 BC), representing figure of Legalism during Warring States period, served as chancellor under Duke Xiao of Qin. He transformed Legalist philosophy into operable state running code, creating the 'Qin System' — the first politician in Chinese history to institutionalize and engineer rule-of-law thought. His reforms enabled Qin to transform from weak to strong, laying foundation for unification of six states. Posterity evaluated: "Lord Shang torn apart but laws not abolished" — his legal system虽严苛但极其有效. He advocated "Law Does Not Favor the Noble, Rope Does Not Bend for Curves", emphasizing equality before law (at least for commoners), and established powerful state machinery through specific systems including 'Wood-Credibility', 'Mutual Responsibility', and 'Merit-Based Nobility'.
